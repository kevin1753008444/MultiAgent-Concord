# Concord Massachusetts Prison Publications (1886 & 1982)

## Document 1: "Our Paper"
**Source:** Massachusetts Reformatory, Warnerville (Concord)  
**Date:** October 30, 1886  
**Primary Objective:** Moral reform, literacy, and character discipline.

### Key Content Pillars
* **The Reformatory Philosophy:** Content is heavily didactic. The "Poet’s Corner" and various "Sense and Nonsense" columns provide moral maxims intended to instill Victorian values, humility, and family loyalty.
* **Intellectual Societies:** Reports on active inmate-led organizations including:
    * **S.S. & L.C. (Scientific, Social, and Literary Club):** Historical and scientific lectures.
    * **Y.M.C.D.S. (Young Men’s Catholic Debating Society):** Moral debates and historical declamations.
* **Educational Features:** Long-form articles on global history (Giuseppe Garibaldi), natural history (Rocky Mountain Sheep), and cultural studies (Life in China).
* **General News:** A curated digest of world events, notably the unveiling of the Statue of Liberty and national labor strikes.

## Document 2: "Concord Community: Inside/Outside"
**Source:** Northeastern Correctional Center (NCC), Concord  
**Date:** March 1982  
**Primary Objective:** Vocational training, community transparency, and reintegration.

### Key Content Pillars
* **Community Integration Events:**
    * **Elderly Outreach:** Inmates funded and prepared a Christmas dinner for 42 local senior citizens, marking a shift toward restorative community interaction.
    * **Spanish Apostolate:** Regular visits from the Spanish community (Mission Church) involving food, music, and religious support.
* **Vocational & Self-Help Programs:**
    * **C.A.R.V.E. (Concord Achievement Rehabilitation Volunteer Experience):** Inmates working in geriatric care at state hospitals.
    * **Lifer Outreach:** Inmates speaking at local high schools (e.g., Littleton High) to educate youth on the realities of prison.
* **Administrative & Critical Commentary:** * Interviews with Superintendent Bob Walsh regarding institutional costs and staff training.
    * Critical reviews of prison literature (*In the Belly of the Beast*) and editorials on the ethics of the furlough program.
