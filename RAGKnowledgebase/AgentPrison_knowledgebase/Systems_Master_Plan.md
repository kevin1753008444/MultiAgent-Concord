# Facility Analysis: MCI Concord (Medium Security)

## 1. General Information
* **Location:** 965 Elm Street, Concord, MA 01742
* **Year Opened:** 1893 (Originally constructed in 1878)
* **Security Level:** Medium
* **Facility Layout:** 528-acre site with 15 buildings in a campus-style layout.
* **Total Square Footage:** 518,202 gsf

---

## 2. Infrastructure & Buildings
The facility is a mix of historical 19th-century architecture and modern utility expansions.

| Building ID | Primary Use | Notable Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bldg. A & B** | Admin / Visiting | Admin is 33,500 gsf; Visiting is 20,748 gsf. |
| **Bldg. C, E, J, L** | Housing | Includes both cell-based and dorm-style units. |
| **Bldg. H** | Gym / School | 64,000 gsf; Includes overflow housing conversions. |
| **Modular Units** | Housing | Wood-frame structures added in the mid-1990s. |
| **Power Plant** | Utilities | Received low-pressure steam renovation in 2004. |

---

## 3. Housing Capacity (2010 Data)
The facility consistently operates above its design baseline, utilizing non-traditional spaces to manage the population.

* **Current Number of Beds:** 1,368
* **CMP Baseline Capacity:** 595
* **Average Daily Population (2009):** 1,303
* **Capacity Issues:** * Significant use of "Over Flow Housing" in gyms and laundry areas.
    * Many areas (like J-7 and L-3) are designated for temporary housing but are not included in formal capacity counts.

---

## 4. Key Challenges & Deficiencies

### **Operational Issues**
* **Crowding:** The visitation center is frequently overcrowded, resulting in visitors being turned away.
* **Security:** Systems vary significantly in age and are not integrated into a single network.
* **Repurposing:** Traditional storage and program areas have been converted to dorm-style housing.

### **Major Deferred Maintenance (Urgent 2010 Requests)**
The facility faces several high-cost infrastructure needs:
* **Security Improvements:** $3,500,000 for site-wide upgrades.
* **Housing Replacements:** $5,500,000 to replace the aging Modular Building units.
* **Environmental/DPH:** $2,500,000 for window replacements (Buildings C & E) and over $1,500,000 for HVAC and ductwork upgrades to meet health standards.
* **Infrastructure:** $1,500,000 for sewer line replacement in E-Building.

---

## 5. Summary Assessment
MCI Concord is an aging facility under high stress. While it serves as a primary intake and classification center, the infrastructure is heavily burdened by an inmate population that is more than double its baseline capacity ($595$ vs $1,303$). This has resulted in a backlog of millions of dollars in deferred maintenance, specifically regarding security integration and environmental health (ventilation and plumbing).
