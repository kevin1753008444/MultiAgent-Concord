# Bachelard's *The Poetics of Space*

## Source

Gaston Bachelard, *The Poetics of Space* (1958). Chapters 1 and 9.

---

## Chapter 1: The House

### Core Thesis

All really inhabited space bears the essence of the notion of home. A building is not an object to be described or measured. It is a container of memory, dream, and emotion. The house shelters daydreaming, protects the dreamer, and allows one to dream in peace. Past, present, and future coexist within the same walls — they interpenetrate through dreams, each deepening the other.

### Key Concepts

**Inhabited space as living body.** The house is not experienced from day to day on the thread of a narrative. Through dreams, the various dwelling-places in our lives co-penetrate and retain the treasures of former days. An entire past comes to dwell in a new house. We bring our lares with us. When memories of other places we have lived in come back to us, we travel to the land of Motionless Childhood — motionless the way all immemorial things are.

**Memory embedded in material.** We live fixations — fixations of happiness. We comfort ourselves by reliving memories of protection. Something closed must retain our memories, while leaving them their original value as images. Memories of the outside world will never have the same tonality as those of home.

**Imagination augments reality.** A sort of attraction for images concentrates them about the house. Imagination augments the values of reality. The daydream deepens to the point where an immemorial domain opens up for the dreamer — beyond man's earliest memory. In the order of values, memory and imagination constitute a community. We are never real historians, but always near poets.

**The original shell.** In every dwelling, even the richest, the first task is to find the original shell. We take root, day after day, in a corner of the world. The house is our corner of the world. It is our first universe, a real cosmos in every sense of the word.

## Chapter 9: The Dialectics of Outside and Inside

### Core Thesis

Outside and inside are not symmetrical. They do not receive the same qualifying epithets. The opposition between them is not geometrical — it is charged with aggression, hesitation, and ambiguity. Inside and outside exchange their dizziness. Sometimes the prison is on the outside. Man is half-open being.

### Key Concepts

**The reversal of inside and outside.** The two spaces of inside and outside exchange their dizziness. A poet who rode endlessly across the South American pampas wrote: the pampa assumed the aspect of a prison for me — a prison that was bigger than the others. Too much space smothers us much more than if there were not enough. The interior can feel vast; the exterior can feel suffocating.

**The loss of geometric homeland.** When the certainty of inside and the distinctness of outside suddenly collapse, intimate space loses its clarity, while exterior space loses its void. The mind has lost its geometrical homeland and the spirit is drifting. We are banished from the realm of possibility.

**Being reduced to noise.** Destroyed by punishment, it was reduced to a noise — a thunderous noise. An immense world still heard it, but it no longer existed, having become simply and solely a noise, which was to rumble on for centuries longer, but was fated to die out completely, as though it had never existed.

**The door as a living threshold.** The door is an entire cosmos of the Half-open. It is one of the primal images — the very origin of a daydream that accumulates desires and temptations. The door scents me, it hesitates. A threshold is a sacred thing. If one were to give an account of all the doors one has closed and opened, of all the doors one would like to re-open, one would have to tell the story of one's entire life. There are two beings in a door — one who opens and one who closes.

**The cell as intimate immensity.** The cell of myself fills with wonder / The white-washed wall of my secret. The poet inhabits the cellular image. Secret being feels that it is guarded more by the whiteness of the lime-wash than by the strong walls. The whiteness of the walls, alone, protects the dreamer's cell. It is stronger than all geometry.

**Being as sensitivity to disturbance.** Being is so sensitive that it is upset by a word. To think that a mere word, a name, suffices to make the dividing walls of your strength come tumbling down. This sort of derangement, which is peculiar to us, must go into our work.

**The room that enters the self.** A slow humility penetrates the room / That dwells in me in the palm of repose. The intimacy of the room becomes our intimacy. We no longer see it. It no longer limits us, because we are in the very ultimate depth of its repose.

## Vocabulary and Phrasing Patterns

### On walls and boundaries

- Walls do not merely enclose — they retain. Something closed must retain our memories.
- The dividing walls of your strength can come tumbling down from a single word.
- The whiteness of walls is stronger than all geometry. It guards more than thick stone.
- Limits are not barriers. They are surfaces where being wants to be both visible and hidden.

### On time and duration

- An entire past comes to dwell. Former days are not gone — they are retained in the walls.
- We travel to the land of Motionless Childhood — motionless the way all immemorial things are.
- A noise that will rumble on for centuries, but is fated to die out completely, as though it had never existed.

### On vulnerability and sensation

- Being is so sensitive that it is upset by a word.
- The level rises outside, and it also rises in you — not in the vessels you control, but in the capillary veins, drawn upward into the furthermost branches of your infinitely ramified existence.
- Night without objects. Window muffled on the outside. Doors carefully closed. Silence in the stair-well, silence in the adjoining rooms, silence up there, on the ceiling.

### On existing and ceasing to exist

- It no longer existed, having become simply and solely a noise.
- Where can one flee, where find refuge? In what shelter can one take refuge? Space is nothing but a horrible outside-inside.
- The spiraled being who, from outside, appears to be a well-invested center, will never reach his center.
- Often it is in the heart of being that being is errancy.

### On doors and openings

- The door scents me, it hesitates.
- On May nights, when so many doors are closed, there is one that is just barely ajar.
- Doors that open on the countryside seem to confer freedom behind the world's back.
- He who opens a door and he who closes it — are they the same being?
