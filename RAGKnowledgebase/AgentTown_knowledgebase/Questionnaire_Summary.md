# MCI-Concord Redevelopment Questionnaire Summary

This document summarizes the feedback from the public hearing and information session held on September 12, 2024. A total of 317 responses were collected via digital questionnaires, paper forms, and email.

---

## 1. Participant Overview
* **Total Respondents**: 317.
* **Primary Demographic**: 284 respondents identified as residents of Concord.
* **Connection to Site**: 241 respondents live nearby, while 236 drive past the site.

---

## 2. Key Areas of Excitement
Respondents expressed optimism regarding the following opportunities:

* **Commercial Growth**: Potential for more diverse food options, retail (like a hardware store), and lower-priced fitness facilities to increase the tax base.
* **Community & Recreation**: Restoration of land for public access, including sports fields for children and improved connections to the Assabet River and local trails.
* **Housing Diversity**: Opportunities for affordable, workforce, and senior/assisted living housing to support moderate-income and lower-income families.
* **Sustainability**: Interest in Net Zero buildings, smart growth, geothermal energy, solar arrays, and protecting native plant species.
* **Infrastructure**: Significant excitement regarding the potential to fix the dangerous nearby rotary and add bike trails across Route 2.

---

## 3. Primary Concerns
Several factors caused hesitation among the respondents:

* **Traffic Congestion**: Fear that increased density will worsen the "horrific" rotary traffic and strain local roads.
* **Town Service Impacts**: Concerns that more housing will overwhelm the public school system, police, sewage systems, and hospitals, which are noted as being at capacity.
* **Commercial Character**: A strong desire to avoid "big box" stores, chain restaurants, and strip malls.
* **Design Quality**: Hesitation regarding "cookie-cutter" designs and the use of cheap materials that do not match the charm of Concord.
* **Process Stalemate**: Worry that disagreement between stakeholders could paralyze progress or that the State might impose its will on the Town.

---

## 4. Future Vision for the Site
When asked what should be developed, respondents prioritized:

* **Mixed-Use Vibrant Spaces**: A blend of open green areas, local businesses (restaurants/cafes), and non-profit offices.
* **Affordable Housing**: Creating a "whole new town center" with multi-family, non-luxury housing that complements West Concord.
* **Historical Preservation**: Retention of the fountain in front of the Overflow Building and the historic former warden's home.
* **Enhanced Access**: Improved roadway safety, ample parking for recreational fields, and better access to public transportation.
